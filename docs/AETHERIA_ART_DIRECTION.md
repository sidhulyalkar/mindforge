# Aetheria Art Direction

## One-sentence visual target

A cathedral-scale techno-magical realm of obsidian, hard-light and rhythmic machinery populated by deliberately over-engineered cyber-mythic enemies and one physically ridiculous, brightly armored block-squire.

## Contrast is the style

The world is not cartoony simply because the protagonist is funny. Keep three rendering languages deliberately distinct:

### Aetheria architecture

- monumental;
- geometric;
- weathered but not dirty-noise everywhere;
- dark basalt/obsidian mass with thin hard-light circuitry;
- cyan/green guild energy versus violet hostile control;
- distant silhouettes substantially larger than gameplay geometry;
- repeating ribs, buttresses, towers, bells, rails and speaker structures provide parallax.

### Prism Squire

- small block anatomy;
- oversized holographic medieval armor;
- high-chroma cyan, rose, gold accents over pale/white armor;
- giant helmet with independent wobble layer;
- weapon intentionally oversized;
- motion uses squash, spring, overshoot and secondary wobble in presentation only;
- readable at distance as a bright compact shape, never a realistic knight.

### Malatract / elite enemy order

- realistic proportions relative to the squire;
- controlled animation;
- darker, narrower palette;
- precise symmetry;
- mechanical articulation and restrained emission;
- visual seriousness should make the protagonist appear even stranger.

## Lighting hierarchy

1. player/enemy contact and immediate danger;
2. coded neural target when relevant;
3. character silhouette and target lock;
4. district landmark;
5. decorative hard-light;
6. ambient bass-reactive detail.

Do not let the whole frame reach maximum emission. A bright Aetherblade needs darkness around it to feel hot.

## Aetherblade

Target construction:

- almost-white inner plasma core;
- cyan sheath;
- thin translucent outer bloom;
- visibly engineered emitter/hilt;
- short bright contact trail;
- fainter secondary afterimage;
- tip cap hotter than outer sheath;
- Sight resonance may lengthen/intensify the manifestation within bounded gameplay truth.

Avoid a single flat neon rectangle. The blade should read as several nested energy densities.

## Hoverbike

The Prism hoverbike is a medieval destrier translated through Aetheria's technology, not a conventional motorcycle with glowing wheels.

Shape language:

- no physical wheels;
- long forward lance/nose;
- compact floating saddle block;
- two lateral hard-light hover rails;
- rear momentum-drive ring;
- downward ion vanes;
- small guild pennant / prism crest;
- strong horizontal silhouette so it reads at speed.

The rider should look slightly too small for it.

Presentation at speed:

- low cyan/rose rail trails;
- restrained reactor pulse;
- subtle pitch/roll lean;
- camera should not add large dynamic FOV changes because stable neural presentation matters;
- boost is shown through denser trail/drive emission rather than huge full-screen bloom.

## Enemy silhouette rules

Every enemy needs one dominant geometric idea:

- Scrap Goblin: low blade wedge;
- Shardsinger: tuning fork;
- Bass Golem: huge chest core;
- Sentry: suspended keel/fins;
- Penitent: asymmetrical executioner;
- Stalker: mantis legs/mandibles;
- Choir Drone: fork + halo;
- Prism Maw: four jaws;
- Veil Reaper: tall split body/scythes;
- Orbit Seraph: orb + orthogonal rings.

Color can reinforce identity but may not be the only differentiator.

## Bass-reactive world

The world may appear rhythmically alive, but audio-reactive presentation must never perturb:

- physics;
- attack timing;
- BCI evidence;
- VEP stimulus timing/luminance;
- collision;
- target lock.

Use bass-reactive motion for decorative strips, banners, skyline windows, speaker membranes and non-coded lights only.

## Capture standard

A good frame should answer, without HUD text:

- where can I move?
- what is trying to hurt me?
- what enemy type is that?
- where is my blade?
- what is the major landmark?

If decorative light prevents any of those answers, remove the light.
