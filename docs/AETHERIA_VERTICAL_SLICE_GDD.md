# Aetheria Vertical Slice GDD

## Product sentence

Mindforge is a third-person techno-fantasy action game where an absurdly buoyant Prism block-squire crosses a world that takes itself painfully seriously, using an oversized Aetherblade, aerial movement, dodge timing, mounted hoverbike combat, and bounded BCI transformations to break Lord Malatract's doctrine of perfect order.

## Tone contract

The world and antagonist are sincere. The protagonist is funny through body language, proportions, momentum, helmet wobble, over-enthusiastic motion, and improbable survival. Do not turn Malatract into a comedian and do not rely on constant quips.

Comedy comes from contrast:

- cathedral-scale grimdark machinery versus a bright block-squire;
- solemn monologues versus uncontrolled physical enthusiasm;
- hyper-engineered enemies versus joyful improvisational movement;
- sacred stillness during a BCI parry versus the Guardian's normal kinetic chaos.

## Core fantasy

Aetheria's magic is generated by momentum, rhythm, and focused intent. Lord Malatract has conquered the realm by imposing deterministic order on its kinetic systems. The Knights of the Prism survive because their movement is difficult to predict and because their highest defensive discipline, the **Stare of Defiance**, converts accepted neural focus into a reflected threat.

Player agency remains conventional. BCI evidence may transform a player-commanded action or accepted defensive state, but it may never originate locomotion, target lock, sword attack, dodge, mount, boost, camera, or traversal.

## Core loop

1. Read a district's geometry and threat mix.
2. Traverse with run, jump, double jump, hover, air dash, shortcut drops, and optional mounted routes.
3. Lock/read enemies by silhouette and telegraph geometry.
4. Commit the Aetherblade.
5. Dodge or reposition after enemy commitment.
6. Punish recovery and earn Flux.
7. Use accepted BCI focus for bounded transformations such as blade resonance or gaze reflection.
8. Reach the next landmark and story beat.

## Player verbs

### On foot

- WASD: movement
- mouse/arrows: orbit camera
- T: target lock
- F/LMB: Aetherblade chain / projectile parry
- Shift/RMB: dodge roll; one air dash while airborne
- Space: jump, double jump; hold while descending for hover
- Q/C/R: advanced abilities retained as secondary systems

### Mounted

- E: mount / dismount nearby Prism hoverbike
- WASD: inertial camera-relative bike movement
- Shift/RMB: boost, not invulnerability
- F/LMB: mounted Aetherblade attack
- T: target lock remains conventional

Mounted mode is mutually exclusive with foot locomotion authority. The Guardian Rigidbody remains the authoritative body; the bike is a visual/interaction mount, not a second physics avatar.

## Signature BCI beat: Stare of Defiance

The Guardian's normal movement is noisy and exuberant. During a qualified focus-parry state the visual character abruptly becomes unnaturally still, helmet wobble settles, the threat alignment becomes explicit, and the reflected orb becomes the payoff.

The BCI path must not create the parry opportunity itself. The player must already be in the relevant defensive/interaction window and the accepted neural event only transforms that state.

## Vertical slice sequence

### 1. Prism Bastion

Purpose: teach movement and establish tonal contrast.

- solemn Aetheria opening transmission;
- block-squire presentation revealed against gigantic hard-light fortification;
- run, jump, double jump, hover, air dash, Aetherblade tutorial;
- first light Scrap-Goblin-style rushers built from the existing Hollow grammar.

### 2. Neon Causeway

Purpose: movement showcase and first hoverbike station.

- long enclosed bridge architecture with strong forward perspective;
- mounted route broad enough for boost and target-lock testing;
- aerial pressure enemies based on Sentry/Shardcaster grammar;
- optional bike use, never required to avoid a soft lock.

### 3. Market of Broken Momentum

Purpose: make Aetheria feel inhabited and introduce mixed-role fights.

- RGB salvage shrines, broken momentum drives, speaker pylons;
- swarm + anchor encounter composition;
- Bass-Golem visual identity maps naturally to the Signal Warden heavy-anchor grammar;
- first explicit gaze-orb reflection demonstration belongs here.

### 4. Choir of Ruined Towers

Purpose: full verticality.

- stacked platforms and tower pockets;
- aerial enemies and ranged crossfire;
- shortcuts visible from below;
- hoverbike parked before or after the vertical core, not on narrow stair geometry.

### 5. Hall of Excessive Gravitas

Purpose: establish Malatract as genuinely dangerous.

- severe symmetry, low-saturation metal, violet control light;
- long monologue transmission;
- player's bright armor and kinetic body language provide the contrast without dialogue jokes.

### 6. Menagerie Crucible

Purpose: ten-enemy combat demo.

- 3 -> 3 -> 4 fixed authored waves;
- compact role-name strip only;
- enemies readable by silhouette, attack geometry, and cadence;
- no boss progression dependency.

### 7. Malatract confrontation

Purpose: combine the complete slice.

- delayed melee strings;
- parryable gaze orbs;
- arena relocation / vertical pressure;
- final phase tests movement, dodge timing, mounted-route knowledge, and focus transformation without requiring live BCI for controller-only qualification.

## Scope gates

For this demo, do not add:

- loot rarity;
- a large weapon inventory;
- procedural open-world generation;
- more than ten ordinary enemy identities;
- BCI-originated player commands;
- another combat scheduler parallel to JourneyEnemyController.

The next quality budget belongs to animation, sound, hit response, enemy readability, world composition, and encounter tuning.
